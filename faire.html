AminoChain

Business & user needs

B2B biomedical marketplace with two distinct users: researchers who needed to find and procure specimens at scale, and donors who needed to trust the process enough to participate. The business needed both sides to work simultaneously to have a marketplace at all. Joined as employee #3 with no design process, no brand, and an engineering prototype.

Collaboration & key decisions

Worked directly with CEO, CTO, and a small engineering team. Key decisions: web-to-native pivot driven by privacy constraints identified with engineering, hiring and directing an external agency for brand, defining data schemas for specimen types in collaboration with engineering rather than letting it be a pure backend decision.

Trade-offs

Two user bases with opposite emotional contexts: clinical and precise for B2B, warm and human for donors, on one brand system. The tension between moving fast for Series A and building something extensible enough to scale to 50+ providers. Showing uncertainty in the AI-adjacent data surfaces versus projecting confidence.

Outcomes

MVP-to-Alpha in 1 month, Beta in 2, GA before Series A. 50+ providers onboarded, 500K+ biosamples in the marketplace. Before: an engineering prototype with no structure. After: a full platform across the procurement dashboard, donor app, and design system.

Process + UI choices

01

Schema work preceded design work

Show the data definition document alongside the specimen search UI. The information architecture, how biosamples were categorized, filtered, and surfaced, was co-designed with engineering, not inherited from the backend.

02

Conditional module architecture

The procurement dashboard was built on a conditional module system: different specimen types surface different data fields. Show how the dashboard adapts to complexity rather than defaulting to a fixed layout.

03

Art direction as strategic choice

The donor app uses a pointillist visual direction: warm, textured, human. This was a deliberate decision to reduce anxiety in a clinical context and build trust with donors contributing to medical research. Not decoration.

Roku

Business & user needs

Consumer TV platform reaching 70M+ active accounts. Core users are everyday viewers: passive discovery, lean-back experience, access across thousands of channels. The business needed to increase streaming hours for OTT content to drive ad revenue while expanding internationally across LATAM, APAC, and Europe.

Collaboration & key decisions

Worked with PM, data science, and engineering across two parallel tracks: Homescreen redesign (experiment-driven) and international expansion. Key decisions: a flexible module system rather than hardcoded layouts, adapting existing patterns for international markets rather than designing from scratch, and surfacing CC and audio track selection as a first-class experience.

Trade-offs

Improving content discovery without disrupting habits for tens of millions of existing users. Designing for markets with different broadcast norms: channel scan in Europe vs. no-scan in the US. Balancing ML-driven content recommendations with user control. Shipping incrementally via A/B experiments rather than a single big redesign.

Outcomes

15-18% increase in streaming hours from A/B experiments on OTT content. Homescreen redesign shipped to millions of users. Expanded across LATAM, APAC, and Europe with localized experiences: closed captions, audio tracks, parental controls, channel scan, and market-specific onboarding.

Process + UI choices

01

Experiment-driven iteration

The Homescreen redesign was shaped by sequential A/B tests: ML content rows, grid customization, moments of delight. Each test informed the next design decision. Show the sequence from hypothesis to shipped layout. The data was the brief.

02

International system design

CC and audio track selection required a data model for complex subtitle and audio combinations across markets. Show the data architecture document alongside the settings UI. The schema work preceded the design work, same pattern as AminoChain.

03

Channel scan as market-specific flow

Channel scan behaves differently by region: antenna in LATAM, cable in the UK, no-scan in the US. The decision was to build an extensible input-type system rather than hardcode market-specific flows. Designing for the system, not the screen.
